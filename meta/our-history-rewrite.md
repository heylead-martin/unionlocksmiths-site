# /our-history/ : rewrite brief and draft copy

## Why this page cannot be search-replaced

The live page uses "Union" as a **meaning-bearing word**, not a label. It appears as:

- "Union Construction Company (華聯建築)" : presented as the English name of the 1934 firm
- "Union grew to become a major contractor..."
- "brings back the Union spirit, unifying the skillsets..."
- "The Union spirit embodies Kew Chan's insistence on..."
- "you'll experience the Union spirit"

Swapping the word mechanically produces "the Wah Luen spirit, unifying the skillsets", which reads as nonsense in English and loses the deliberate pun on "union" meaning "unifying". Every other page survives a find and replace. This one needs writing.

## The strategic upside, and it is a real one

Here is the thing worth telling Kenneth.

The page currently says the 1934 company was 華聯建築, rendered in English as "Union Construction Company". **華聯 is Wah Luen.** Those are the same two characters. The Union brand was built on a *translation* of the characters. Wah Luen is the *sound of the characters themselves*.

So the rename is not a retreat from the heritage story. It moves closer to it. The old page had to explain a translation. The new page just states the name.

That reframes the whole exercise for Kenneth. He is not losing a brand he built, he is recovering the one his grandfather actually used. It is also the strongest possible answer to the natural client worry, which is "we are throwing away our story."

## One caveat you need to resolve before publishing

The live page asserts the firm was called "Union Construction Company" in English. If that is genuinely how the company wrote its own name on letterhead and contracts in Hong Kong, then Wah Luen is a romanization we are choosing, not the historical English name, and the copy must not claim otherwise.

The draft below is written to be true either way. It says 華聯 is the name and Wah Luen is how those characters sound, which is simply accurate. It does not claim "this is how they wrote it in 1934."

If Kenneth produces old documents showing a different romanization, that spelling wins over the standard one and the copy gets adjusted. Worth one direct question to him. Do not publish a heritage claim you cannot support, particularly on a page that will sit behind a trademark filing.

## Draft copy

### Our History

*(image: assets/founder.jpg, alt: "Kew Chan (陳大球, 1882 to 1968), founder of 華聯")*

Our story begins in pre-war Hong Kong, with Kew Chan (陳大球, 1882 to 1968, also known as Chan Tai-kau), a Hakka entrepreneur from the Huiyang prefecture of Guangdong. Around 1934 he founded 華聯建築. In Cantonese, those first two characters are read Wah Luen.

Chan's rule was simple and, by the accounts that reached us, non-negotiable. Every item had to be installed the correct way. No corners cut. If it was not right, it was done again.

That standard built a business. Between the 1940s and the 1970s, 華聯 grew into a major contractor on Hong Kong public works, constructing, maintaining and repairing roads and bridges. The family sold the company in the 1980s.

Almost fifty years later, the name has been taken up again in Singapore, this time by professional, certified locksmiths.

We chose to carry the name in its original form. 華聯, Wah Luen. It is the name as the characters have always sounded, and it is ours to hold rather than borrowed from anyone else.

What we took from it is the standard, not the sentiment. A lock fitted correctly the first time. An honest assessment of what a door actually needs. Work you do not have to think about again.

That is the inheritance worth keeping.

## Notes for the build

- **Do not** repeat the word "spirit" from the old copy. It was carrying the pun and without it the phrase is empty.
- The line "ours to hold rather than borrowed from anyone else" is a quiet nod to why the change happened. It is honest, it reads as confidence, and it never mentions trademarks. Cut it if Kenneth prefers, it is not load-bearing.
- Keep the founder image. It is the most valuable asset on the site and it is not brand-specific.
- 華聯 should be set in a proper CJK typeface, matching whatever the logo redraw settles on. Check the strokes at full size.
- New meta description for this page specifically. Every page on the current site shares one identical description, which is a genuine SEO defect worth fixing during the migration rather than after.

**Suggested title tag:** `Our History | Wah Luen Locksmiths Singapore`
**Suggested meta description:** `From 華聯建築 in 1934 Hong Kong to commercial locksmithing in Singapore. The standard Kew Chan set, carried forward under the original name.`

## Knock-on: the About page

`/about/` almost certainly reuses "the Union spirit" phrasing. Read it alongside this one and rewrite the overlapping paragraphs together, so the two pages do not contradict each other on where the name comes from.

The homepage line "independent of any lock brand" and the asterisked note "We are not a service centre for any lock brand, nor do we claim to have any such affiliation" should **stay**. Given the history here, that disclaimer is now doing real work, and it is worth keeping prominent rather than in fine print.
